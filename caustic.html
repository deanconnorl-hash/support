How it works

Drag one finger anywhere on the screen. That lays down fluid, and light bends toward whatever is thicker — the way a heat haze bends the road behind it.

So the side you draw on decides which way the beam goes. To pull light down, stroke below it. To lift it up, stroke above it. Getting that backwards is the single most common reason a level will not come out.

Common questions

The ring is a colour and nothing happens when I hit it.

A ring drawn in one colour accepts only that wavelength. Pointing the whole beam at it does nothing, however bright it looks.

Each colour bends by a different amount, so the beam has to be turned hard enough to pull the spectrum apart, and then the right band landed on its own. Violet bends hardest and swings furthest. Red bends least and stays nearest the straight path — so a red ring usually needs a gentler touch, not a stronger one, which is the opposite of what it feels like.

My light has gone dim, or disappeared.

Fluid is glass, and light loses a little of itself crossing it. A few deliberate strokes are a lens. A screen covered in fluid is fog, and the beam can fade out entirely before it reaches anything — even while the bar at the bottom says you have plenty left to spend.

If that happens, tap the curved arrow at the top right to start the level again. It starts clean.

The ring keeps moving, or my glass keeps fading.

That is the sixth chapter, Tide, working as intended. Some rings drift back and forth, so the beam has to follow them rather than be aimed once. On some levels the fluid fades on its own, so a lens has to be kept fed while you hold the light on the ring.

Watch a moving ring make one full trip before you touch the screen. It travels in a straight line at a steady pace, so you can time it.

Something dark keeps crossing the light.

That is the seventh chapter, Eclipse. The dark circles that swallow light move back and forth on a steady path, so a route that is blocked now will be open again in a moment.

Some routes are blocked far less often than others. A void spends most of its time near the ends of its swing and hurries through the middle, so watch one full swing and aim for the road it mostly leaves alone.

How do I use a mirror?

Put your finger on the mirror and drag round it. It turns about the small dot in its middle, and the faint circle shows how far it reaches. Turning a mirror uses no glass, but it does count as a stroke for the medals.

Mirrors throw light off at the same angle it arrived, so they can send it somewhere glass never could: over a void, into a corner, or straight back the way it came. Glass still works alongside them - some mirrors sit out of the light's way until you bend the light onto them.

The light itself keeps swinging.

That is the ninth chapter, Beacon. The source sweeps back and forth like a lighthouse. It hurries through the middle of its swing and lingers at each end, so aim for where it lingers - a lens placed there gets a long, steady look at the ring.

I am stuck. Is there a hint?

Yes, on every level and every daily puzzle. Tap the small light bulb at the top right. The level starts over and a ghost finger shows a way to solve it: where to drag, and which way to turn a mirror.

Every hint is a solution that was actually played through the game's own physics, so it works - but your finger is not the ghost's, so watch where it goes and how fast, then do it yourself.

What is the daily puzzle?

A new puzzle every day, the same one for everyone, at the top of the level list. It changes at midnight where you are and needs no internet. Early in the week they are gentle; by the weekend they combine two ideas at once. Solve it on consecutive days to build a streak.

What do the coloured circles and dashed mirrors do?

A coloured circle is glass that lets one colour through and stops the rest. Put across the beam near its start, it changes what the beam is - a red beam bends least of all, a violet one most.

A dashed mirror is half-silvered: half the light goes through and half reflects, so one beam can light two rings.

How do prisms work?

That is the thirteenth chapter, Prism. A prism is a solid triangle of heavy glass. White light goes in and comes out as a fan, each colour leaving in its own direction - violet bent most, red least.

A prism with a dot in its middle turns when you drag round it, like a mirror, and turning it swings the whole fan. Turn it too far and the glass holds the light in and drops it out of the bottom; violet, the heaviest, is the first colour it keeps. The fluid still bends the fan once it is out.

A mirror is moving on its own.

That is the fourteenth chapter, Clockwork. Those mirrors rock back and forth by themselves and cannot be turned. Like the lighthouse in Beacon, they hurry through the middle of their swing and linger at each end, so the reflection rests longest there. Watch one full rock, then put your glass where the light will be when it lingers.

A ring is drawn in two colours.

That is the fifteenth chapter, Chord. A two-coloured ring wants both colours at the same time - not either one. White light does not count, so in these levels the colours start apart, and the puzzle is bringing them back together on the same small circle. The names of both colours are written beneath it.

A ring is drawn as a broken circle.

That is the sixteenth chapter, Dark. A broken ring is the opposite of every other ring: it wants no light at all. While a beam is on one, nothing else fills, so a wide, hopeful aim starts to cost something. A filament brushing its edge is fine - it answers light pointed at it, not light that grazes it.

A wall vanished when I lit a ring.

That is the seventeenth chapter, Gate. Those walls are doors, and each one opens when the ring it belongs to is lit. It is the only thing in the game that puts a level in an order: until the first ring is done, the far half of the board cannot be reached at all.

A mirror will not turn, but it slides.

That is the eighteenth chapter, Rail. A mirror on a rail has a faint track drawn through it with a stop at each end. Turning a mirror changes where the light goes next; sliding one changes where the light meets it, which moves the whole reflected beam without changing its angle. Drag it along the track.

A ring keeps changing colour.

That is the nineteenth chapter, Beat. The ring wants one colour at a time and changes on its own every few seconds, and it wants that colour on its own - a whole fan laid across it gives nothing. Each colour counts once, so holding one on the ring while the cycle comes round again does nothing either. Watch it for a cycle before you pour.

The light bounced off the edge of the board.

That is the twentieth chapter, Hall. In these levels the four edges are silvered, so light that would have left comes back, a little dimmer. Overshooting stops being waste, and rings that nothing could reach before become reachable the long way round.

What is the small diamond on some levels?

It marks a chapter's finale: its last and biggest level, asking for everything the chapter taught. There is a Game Center achievement for beating every one.

My glass keeps moving on its own.

That is the twelfth chapter, Current. The glass is carried by a steady drift, or round and round a whirlpool, so a lens has to be laid upstream of where you want it and allowed to arrive. The light itself is not carried - only the glass bending it.

Where can I see how I am doing?

Tap the line at the top of the level list - "12 of 152 lit" - to open the Journal: levels, chapters and finales finished, medals and light touches, rings lit, daily puzzles, streaks and your fastest daily, your best Endless run and your best daily run, the chapters you have raced and your fastest one, and your time with the light.

What are Endless and Free Play?

Two more ways to play, at the top of the level list and open from the start.

Endless gives you one ring at a time. Each ring you light gives some glass back, and the rings slowly get smaller, pickier about colour, and start to move. The deeper you go, the more often a ring arrives with something beside it - a void to go round, a current, a mirror to turn, or coloured glass over the light. The run ends when your glass is gone and nothing is left charging. Your best score is kept.

Today's Run is the same run of Endless for everyone who plays it that day: the same rings, in the same order, so the only thing that differs is how far you get. Game Center lists how everyone did.

Free Play has no rings and no limit. It is just the fluid and the light. Tap "Light" at the bottom to switch between setups - one beam, two crossing, mirrors, a lighthouse, and a whirlpool. The curved arrow at the top right clears the glass, and the share button beside it saves or sends a picture of what you have made.

Why are some level numbers gold, silver or bronze?

That is how you solved them. Finish a level in a single stroke and it turns gold; two strokes is silver; more is bronze. Turning a mirror counts as a stroke. A few levels where more strokes are provably needed give gold for that number instead. Only your best counts, so replaying a level can never take a medal away.

What is the small mark on the glass bar?

It is the light-touch mark. Finish a level with the bar still above it - having laid no more glass than that - and the level earns a light touch, shown as a small pale bead under its number. Every mark was set from a solution actually played on the game's physics, with room to spare.

How do I get the other glass colours?

With gold medals. The colour picker is at the bottom of the level list; each colour shows how many golds it needs. Only the glass changes colour - the light keeps its true colours, because the colours are the puzzle.

I have run out of fluid.

The bar along the bottom is your budget, and it is measured in how far your finger has travelled — not in strokes and not in seconds. Short, placed strokes go a very long way. Most levels are meant to fall to one or two.

Once the glass is gone and nothing is still charging, the game offers to start the level again. The curved arrow at the top right does the same thing at any time.

I am stuck and I want to move on.

Tap the counter at the top right — the "12 / 152" — to open the level select. You can go back to anything you have finished, and you can move past a level that has beaten you. Nothing is locked away for good.

There is no sound.

Caustic mixes politely with whatever else you are playing, which means it also respects the silent switch. If your phone is on silent, the app is silent. Flip the switch, or raise the volume while the app is open.

The sound is one tone that swells as a ring fills, so it is quiet by design until light is actually landing on a target.

Sound and vibration can each be switched off at the bottom of the level list. With sound off, the app does not use audio at all.

I find some ring colours hard to tell apart.

Every ring that wants one colour has its name written just beneath it. That is on by default; the switch is at the bottom of the level list.

With the system's Differentiate Without Colour setting on, medals also carry a letter. Increase Contrast brightens the game's text, Reduce Motion calms its transitions, and larger text sizes enlarge the notes and menus. VoiceOver reads each level's rings and where they sit, and lets your finger through to the glass.

Is there a leaderboard?

If you use Game Center, your best Endless run goes on its leaderboard, your first solve of each daily puzzle goes on Today's Light, and there are achievements to earn. Tap Today's Times after solving the daily puzzle to see how you compare with everyone who solved the same one. Watching the hint keeps your solve and your streak but posts no time that day.

It is entirely optional: the game never asks you to sign in, and the Game Center button at the bottom of the level list is the only way in.

Does it work on iPad?

Yes. The playfield is a fixed shape on every device, letterboxed into whatever screen it is given, so a level is exactly the same puzzle on an iPad as on a phone. If you are signed in to iCloud, your progress follows you between your devices.

Is there anything to buy, or to sign up for?

No. One purchase: 152 levels, a daily puzzle, Endless and Free Play. No advertising, no in-app purchases, no account, and nothing collected about you. The daily puzzle works offline. See the privacy policy.
